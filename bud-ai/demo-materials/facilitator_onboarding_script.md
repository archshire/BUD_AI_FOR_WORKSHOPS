# Facilitator Onboarding Script — "Meet Your AI Partner"
**Runtime: ~5 minutes | Setting: start of a live multilingual workshop, all participants joined, before the first task begins**

---

### [0:00–0:45] Welcome + framing the problem

*(Warm, conversational tone. Facilitator is on camera/mic, participants can see the workshop room.)*

"Hi everyone, welcome — before we dive into today's session, I want to spend two minutes on something new: each of you has an AI Partner working alongside you in this workshop. I want you to know what it does, what it doesn't do, and how to use it, so it's not a mystery halfway through.

Here's the problem it's solving. In a room like this, with people speaking different languages, translation gets you *words* — but it doesn't always get you *understanding*. You can get a perfect translation of an instruction and still miss what it actually means. Or you can 'understand' a teammate's translated message and still walk away with the wrong idea of what they meant. And from where I'm sitting, with this many of you at once, I genuinely can't tell who's stuck and who's fine just by watching.

That's the gap the AI Partner fills. Not by replacing me, and not by replacing each other — it's there to help us actually reach each other."

---

### [0:45–2:00] ME — your private partner

"So here's how it works, starting with what's just for you.

Each of you has a **private AI Partner** — think of it as a quiet assistant sitting next to you, not a proctor. As you work on a task, if something you say suggests you might have missed the point, it won't announce that to the room. It'll just check in with you, privately, something like: *'Quick check — where are you at with this?'*

You can answer however's true: 'I'm following,' 'I'm a bit lost, catch me up,' or even 'I disagree with the direction' — that's a completely valid answer too. Disagreement isn't an error state.

Two things I want you to really trust here:

First — **it will never just decide you're confused and leave it at that.** It asks. Your answer is what it goes on.

Second — **you can correct it, and it'll drop it.** If it checks in and you say 'no, I've got this, this is my answer' — it accepts that. It doesn't keep pinging you about something you've already told it isn't a problem.

If you *are* stuck, it can offer help in your own language, right there, privately. No one else sees that exchange unless you choose to share it."

---

### [2:00–3:15] US — when translation isn't enough between two of you

"Now, the second layer — this one's for when you're talking to each other.

Translation happens automatically here, at the end of a full thought, not word-by-word — so it won't be choppy. But sometimes even a good translation doesn't tell you what someone *meant*. Maybe the phrasing was ambiguous, maybe it's a cultural reference, maybe the tone got lost.

When that happens, you can literally ask your AI Partner: *'What did they mean by that?'* It'll look at what was actually said, how it was translated, and the context of what you're working on, and give you its best read — with appropriate hedging, not false confidence. Something like: *'I think they may mean X rather than Y, based on the context. Want me to help confirm with them?'*

If you say yes, it can help route a quick confirmation back to the other person — but it's a go-between, not a referee. It doesn't get to decide what your teammate meant. Only they can tell you that. And importantly — if the two of you actually did understand each other and you just genuinely disagree, it's not going to relabel that as a 'misunderstanding' that needs fixing. Disagreement between people who understand each other is allowed to just... be disagreement."

---

### [3:15–4:15] THE ROOM — what I, as facilitator, actually see

"Now, from my side. I'm not watching a transcript of everyone's private conversations — I want to be upfront about that, because it matters.

What I *can* do is ask my dashboard something like 'how's everyone doing?' and get back a synthesized read on the room — for example, 'a few participants across two groups seem to be hitting the same snag on this task.' It'll tell me what it's seeing, how confident it is, what's already been tried, and what it recommends — but the call is always mine. I can act on it, ignore it, or ask for more.

If I ever need more detail than that — say, an actual private conversation — the system asks *you* for permission first. If you say no, that's final. No workaround, no paraphrase-and-share. That boundary is built into the app itself, not just something I've promised you."

---

### [4:15–5:00] Close — the norms, and what to actually do

"So, practically, here's all you need to remember:

- Speak or type in whichever language you're most comfortable in.
- If your AI Partner checks in on you, just answer honestly — 'fine,' 'lost,' or 'I disagree' are all fair answers.
- If a translated message from someone else feels off, ask your AI Partner what they might have meant before assuming the worst.
- Your private chats stay private unless you choose to share them.
- And if something's just not working — say so out loud to me directly. The AI supports this workshop; it doesn't run it. I do, and so do you.

Alright — let's get started."

---

**[Facilitator notes, not spoken]**
- Total spoken word count target: ~700–750 words for a natural 5-minute pace (~145–150 wpm).
- If running long, the ME and US sections (the largest chunks) are the easiest to trim — the THE ROOM and closing sections carry the trust/privacy commitments and shouldn't be cut.
- Pause after "Alright — let's get started" for the first real task prompt to begin.
