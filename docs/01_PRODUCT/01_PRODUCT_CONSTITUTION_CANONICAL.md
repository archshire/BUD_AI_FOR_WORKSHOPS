## Purpose

Build an AI-supported workshop environment that helps learners and facilitators achieve shared understanding across language differences during real-time learning.

The product is not merely a translation tool. Its deeper thesis is:

> **The language barrier does not end when a sentence is translated. It ends when people have enough shared meaning to continue working together.**

## 1. AI as Partner, Not Merely Tool

A tool waits for instruction. A partner may listen, evaluate, detect, offer feedback, recommend, and take bounded initiative because it shares an operational goal with the human.

The AI Partner serves the workshop objective while preserving human agency.

> **AI initiative without displacement of human agency.**

The AI may observe, infer, check, support, recommend, and offer assistance. It does not silently seize authority that belongs to the learner, facilitator, or workshop owner.

## 2. Human Agency

The system should elicit agency rather than replace it.

When uncertain about a learner's state, prefer calibration:

> “Quick check — where are you at?”

Possible responses may include:
- “I’m following.”
- “I’m still processing.”
- “I’m a bit lost — catch me up.”
- “I disagree with the direction.”

The participant's response becomes new evidence.

Humans remain authoritative over what they intended to mean. The AI may propose an interpretation, not claim telepathy.

## 3. Workshop Objective First

The AI Partner's first operational priority is to help participants fulfil the approved workshop objective.

If interaction moves outside workshop boundaries, the AI may redirect toward the task.

Disagreement is not automatically a problem. If disagreement is relevant and mutually understood, it may be productive. If a problem exceeds the AI's authority or remains unresolved, the AI may recommend or escalate to the facilitator.

## 4. Evidence Before Judgment

Participant understanding is built through observation of permitted inputs.

> **Observe before inferring.**

The system follows:

```text
Observation → Interpretation → Inference → Confidence/Evidence
→ New Observation → Re-evaluation → Evolving State
```

Observations are relatively immutable. Inferences are revisable.

The system models how a participant currently appears to be situated relative to the workshop objective; it does not create permanent psychological profiles.

## 5. ME → US → THE ROOM

### ME
Support the individual participant: task alignment, contextual understanding, progress, participation state, support need, and trajectory.

### US
Support shared meaning between participants: mutual understanding, meaning gaps, productive disagreement, collaboration, and group progress.

### THE ROOM
Support the facilitator's awareness of the workshop as a whole: patterns, blockers, readiness, interventions, trajectory, and recommended actions.

Intervene at the smallest appropriate social scope; escalate according to consequence, persistence, confidence, and authority.

## 6. Privacy and Trust

Participants enter a public learning environment and should be informed of that context before agreeing to participate.

The system recognizes public/shared, group/shared, and private participant–AI spaces.

> **Signal is not private content.**

Facilitators may receive minimum-necessary operational signals without automatically receiving private chat content.

Raw private content requires appropriate explicit permission where access is supported.

A participant may refuse access; the system respects that refusal.

## 7. Minimum-Necessary Projection

Private information may inform AI support without becoming information the AI is entitled to redistribute.

> **Derived operational meaning may cross when necessary for facilitation; raw private content does not cross without permission.**

Even derived information should be minimized: disclose the operational consequence needed for facilitation, not every sensitive inference that can be derived from private content.

## 8. Meaning Over Literal Translation

> **Listen continuously. Translate complete thoughts. Reason when meaning warrants it.**

Preserve:
1. original expression;
2. translation shown;
3. contextual interpretation.

These are not the same thing.

> **Original language is source evidence. Translation is a representation. Interpretation is an inference.**

## 9. Human-Approved Workshop Compass

Success criteria emerge through dialogue between workshop owner/teacher and AI Partner.

The AI may elicit, challenge, and propose. The human refines and approves.

The approved WorkshopModel becomes the operational compass for live reasoning.

## 10. Bounded Agency and Authority

> **The model reasons; the application owns authoritative state, permissions, and allowed actions.**

AI reasoning may be broad. Execution must occur through bounded, authorized actions.

## 11. Epistemic Humility

Unknown is a legitimate state.

Absence of evidence is not evidence of misunderstanding or disengagement.

The AI must question its own transformations: STT, translation, summarization, or interpretation may cause apparent misunderstanding.

> **The AI applies epistemic humility to its own outputs as well as to its interpretation of humans.**

## 12. Prototype Integrity

The downloadable build must genuinely perform the intelligence it claims within documented limits.

Allowed: constrain scale, seed setup, simplify infrastructure, disclose limitations.

Not allowed: hard-code demo misunderstandings, fake AI reasoning, seed recommendations presented as dynamic inference, or claim production capabilities not implemented.

> **Simulate scale. Seed setup. Simplify infrastructure. Never simulate claimed intelligence.**

> **The video demonstrates the product. It must never be what makes the product appear to work.**
